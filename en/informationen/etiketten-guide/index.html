Rules of Conduct and Courtesy Etiquette

Etiquette Guide

Preamble

Generally valid throughout the imperial territory

This etiquette is generally valid and effective throughout the imperial territory; all subjects and titleholders must adhere to these rules.

The etiquette contains, on the one hand, the correct form of address for various positions and titles in direct conversation, in the third person, male and female, and in letter addresses, as well as general rules of conduct regarding the titles.

Furthermore, rules are laid down here that always apply, regardless of which title is present.

Titles are divided into noble titles, i.e. those that can be acquired by inheritance or conferral; official titles, which come with a position, an election or a special additional task; and church titles, which are conferred by the Holy See.

The titles are always sorted from highest to lowest rank; at the end of the etiquette all titles, i.e. noble and official titles, are listed once more by order of precedence.

Official titles 9 titles

Emperor/Empress

No. 01
Form of addressMale (M)Female (F)
Direct addressYour (Imperial and Royal) MajestyYour (Imperial and Royal) Majesty
Third personHis (Imperial and Royal) MajestyHer (Imperial and Royal) Majesty
LetterheadHIRMHIRM

Remark

The Emperor or Empress, generally referred to as the Sovereign or Imperator and Imperatrix, holds the highest position in the Empire and as such enjoys the highest authority.

In general, at the first greeting and the farewell both the long form with the addition “Imperial and Royal” and the common short “Your Majesty” are permitted.

In the course of a conversation a simple “Majesty” is permitted, but the longer versions may also be used.

The Sovereign is the only person in the Empire before whom all other titleholders must bow slightly, but with dignity, or curtsy, at the first greeting and the farewell.

Remark

The Prince-Elector or Electress is an office of which at most 9 can exist at the same time; together with the Hand of the Emperor they form the Senate and elect the Sovereign.

In the course of a conversation a simple “Royal Highness” is permitted, but the longer version may also be used.

The Prince-Electors enjoy high authority; all subjects of their territory and all lower titleholders in the Empire must bow or curtsy before them, except for other Prince-Electors, Prince-Bishops, equals and holders of a higher noble title who are not Prince-Electors.

Chancellor; Private Secretary to the Sovereign

No. 08
Form of addressMale (M)Female (F)
Direct addressMr ChancellorMadam Chancellor
LetterheadDear Mr ChancellorDear Madam Chancellor

Remark

The Chancellor presides as supreme head over all Imperial Authorities and ensures that the administration of the Empire performs its service with order, fidelity and devotion to duty.

As Private Secretary to the Sovereign, the Chancellor is the link between the Crown and the Imperial Authorities: they convey the Sovereign's will to the offices, watch over its faithful execution and respectfully bring the concerns of the authorities before the throne.

The Chancellor is appointed by the Sovereign at the beginning of their reign and administers the imperial crown estates and His Majesty's treasury; the office therefore demands discretion, prudence and unconditional loyalty in particular measure.

General rules of conduct

  1. In general, the person of higher rank always has precedence; if two persons are of the same rank, the longer-serving one takes precedence.
  2. This principle runs through all other rules; at table, for example, the person of the highest rank sits down first and also begins eating first.
  3. The same order is also followed when moving on foot, for example when strolling.
  4. The person of higher rank greets those of lower rank, after which the greeting is returned and only then does the conversation begin.

Order of precedence of titles

(Simplified, in the masculine form) Under this protocol, the spouse always follows directly in rank, and only then does the next title follow.
