Plenary hall

The Seating Plan of the Reichstag

The plenary hall of the Reichstag building in Greater Berlin is laid out as a semicircle. At the front the sovereign sits on the throne, beside him the presidium of the Reichstag; the imperial administration bench lies to the left, the Senate bench to the right, and in front of them the plenum of the constituent states.

Each sovereign leads a delegation of six seats: himself and five delegates who may attend the sessions. The delegations are seated from west to east according to the location of their capital.

Hover over a seat or choose a delegation in the list to highlight it.

  • Thron (der Kaiser)1

Die Bänke

  • Throne: Where the director of a parliament would normally sit, the monarch sits. The Reichstag has no director.
  • Presidium: The President of the Reichstag chairs the sessions; the office rotates among the delegates every three months.
  • Imperial Administration Bench (left, 18 seats): the Crown Chancellor and the heads of the imperial authorities. In front sit the Crown Chancellor and the heads of the authorities for security and order as well as of the Treasury and Currency Office, the Consulate General and the College of Heralds; behind them the Crown Estates, the imperial offices, the Central Bank and the Imperial Treasury. There are no further offices.
  • Senate Bench (right, 8 seats): Seats for the Prince-Electors of the Senate. The Emperor sits on the throne.
  • Plenum (120 seats): 17 delegations of the sovereigns, 2 reserved delegations for territories under imperial representation and 1 free reserve block.

Reserved and free seats

Delegations are held in reserve for the constituent states under imperial representation (Governorate of Baden, Governorate-General of Venice). Should such a territory receive a sovereign again, his delegation takes the reserved place without changing the seating plan.

The free reserve block at the eastern end is held for a constituent state created in future. A new sovereign receives his six seats there.
