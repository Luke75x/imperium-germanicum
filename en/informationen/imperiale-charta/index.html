Preamble

Preamble

Conscious of its responsibility before God, mankind and the Sovereign, and inspired by the will to serve the peace of the world as an equal member of a united Empire, the people, by virtue of its authority, has given itself this Charter.

The subjects in the 9 Electorates and dozens of further territorial bodies have completed the unity and freedom of the Empire. This Charter thus applies to the entire people of the Imperium Germanicum.

Section I

Fundamental Rights

§ 1

Human Dignity

(1) Human dignity is inviolable. To respect and protect it is the duty of all state authority.

(2) The people of the Empire therefore acknowledge inviolable and inalienable human rights as the basis of every human community, of peace and of justice in the world.

(3) The following fundamental rights shall bind the legislature, the executive and the judiciary as directly applicable law

§ 2

Free Development, Life and Liberty

(1) Everyone has the right to the free development of their personality, insofar as they do not violate the rights of others and do not offend against the order of the Charter or fall under lèse-majesté.

(2) Everyone has the right to life and physical integrity. Freedom of the person is inviolable. These rights may be interfered with only pursuant to a law.

§ 3

Equality of Rights

Men and women have equal rights. The state promotes the actual implementation of equal rights for women and men and works towards the elimination of existing disadvantages.

§ 4

Prohibition of Discrimination

No one may be disadvantaged or favoured because of their sex, parentage, race, language, homeland and origin, faith or religious opinions. No one may be disadvantaged because of a disability.

§ 5

Freedom of Faith and Conscience

(1) Freedom of faith and conscience and freedom of religious and ideological creed are inviolable.

(2) The undisturbed practice of religion is guaranteed.

§ 6

Art and Science

Art and science, research and teaching are free. Freedom of teaching does not release anyone from loyalty to the Sovereign.

§ 7

Freedom of Assembly

All subjects have the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, without notification or permission.

§ 8

Prohibited Associations

Associations whose purposes or activities contravene the criminal laws, or which are directed against the Sovereign or against the idea of monarchy, are prohibited.

§ 9

Acquisition of Citizenship

In principle, anyone may become a citizen of the Imperium Germanicum who has been approved by the Customs Administration and has signed an application for membership.

§ 10

Tax as Membership Fee

All citizens of the Imperium Germanicum pay a tax as a membership fee to finance the state.

Details are governed by a resolution of the Imperial Diet.

§ 11

Withdrawal

Every citizen remains free to leave the Imperium Germanicum at any time without notice.

§ 12

School System

(1) The entire school system is under the supervision of the state.

(2) Parents and guardians have the right to decide whether the child shall receive religious instruction.

(3) Religious instruction shall form part of the regular curriculum in public schools, with the exception of non-denominational schools. Without prejudice to the state's right of supervision, religious instruction shall be given in accordance with the tenets of the religious communities. No teacher may be obliged against their will to give religious instruction.

(4) The right to establish private schools is guaranteed. Private schools that serve as alternatives to public schools require the approval of the state and are subject to the laws of the constituent states. Approval shall be given when private schools are not inferior to public schools in their educational aims, their facilities or the professional training of their teaching staff, and when segregation of pupils according to the means of their parents is not encouraged thereby. Approval shall be withheld if the economic and legal position of the teaching staff is not adequately assured.

(5) A private elementary school shall be approved only if the education authority finds that it serves a special pedagogical interest or if, on the application of parents or guardians, it is to be established as a non-denominational, denominational or ideological school and a public elementary school of this type does not exist in the municipality.

§ 13

Duties of Service

(1) Men who have reached the age of eighteen may be required to serve in the armed forces, in the Customs Administration or in civil protection.

(2) Any person who refuses, on grounds of conscience, to render military service involving the use of arms may be required to perform alternative service. The duration of alternative service shall not exceed the duration of military service. Details shall be regulated by a law, which shall not interfere with the freedom to make a decision in accordance with the dictates of conscience, and which shall also provide for the possibility of alternative service not connected with units of the armed forces or of the Customs Administration.

(3) Persons liable to military service who are not called upon to render service pursuant to paragraph 1 or 2 may, under martial law, be assigned by or pursuant to a law to specific occupations involving civilian services for defence purposes, including the protection of the civilian population; they may be assigned to public employment only for the purpose of discharging police functions or such other sovereign functions of public administration as can be discharged only by persons employed in the public service. The employment relationships referred to in the first sentence may be established with the armed forces, in the area of military procurement, or with public administrative authorities; assignments to employment connected with supplying and servicing the civilian population are permissible only to meet its vital needs or to guarantee its protection.

(4) If, under martial law, the need for civilian services in the civilian health system or in stationary military hospitals cannot be met on a voluntary basis, women between the ages of eighteen and fifty-five may be called upon to render such services by or pursuant to a law. Under no circumstances may they be required to render service involving the use of arms.

(5) In preparation for the services referred to in paragraph 3 that require special knowledge or skills, participation in training courses may be made compulsory by or pursuant to a law. The first sentence shall not apply to that extent.

(6) If, under martial law, the need for workers in the areas specified in the second sentence of paragraph 3 cannot be met on a voluntary basis, the right of citizens to abandon their occupation or place of work may be restricted by or pursuant to a law in order to meet this need. Before martial law takes effect, the first sentence of paragraph 5 shall apply mutatis mutandis.

§ 14

Inviolability of the Home

(1) The home is inviolable.

(2) Searches may be authorised only by a judge or, when time is of the essence, by other authorities designated by the laws, and may be carried out only in the manner therein prescribed.

(3) If particular facts justify the suspicion that any person has committed an especially serious crime specifically defined by a law, technical means of acoustical surveillance of any home in which the suspect is supposedly staying may be employed pursuant to judicial order for the purpose of prosecuting the offence, provided that alternative methods of investigating the matter would be disproportionately difficult or unproductive. The authorisation shall be for a limited time. The order shall be issued by the Imperial Court of Justice. When time is of the essence, it may also be issued by a single judge.

(4) To avert acute dangers to public safety, especially dangers to life or to the public, technical means of surveillance of the home may be employed only pursuant to judicial order. When time is of the essence, such measures may also be ordered by other authorities designated by a law; a judicial decision shall subsequently be obtained without delay.

(5) If technical means are contemplated solely for the protection of persons officially deployed in a home, the measure may be ordered by an authority designated by a law. The information thereby obtained may be otherwise used only for purposes of criminal prosecution or to avert danger, and only if the legality of the measure has been previously determined by a judge; when time is of the essence, a judicial decision shall subsequently be obtained without delay.

(6) The Senate Administration shall inform the Imperial Diet monthly as to the use of technical means pursuant to paragraph 3 and, within the competence of the Empire, pursuant to paragraph 4 and, insofar as judicial approval is required, pursuant to paragraph 5.

(7) Interferences and restrictions shall otherwise be permissible only to avert a danger to the public or to the life of an individual, or, pursuant to a law, to confront an acute danger to public safety and order, in particular to relieve a housing shortage, to combat the danger of an epidemic or to protect young persons at risk

§ 15

Property, Inheritance and Expropriation

(1) Property and the right of inheritance are guaranteed. Their content and limits shall be defined by the laws.

(2) Property entails obligations. Its use shall also serve the public good.

(3) Expropriation shall only be permissible for the public good and the security of the Empire. It may only be ordered by or pursuant to a law that determines the nature and extent of compensation. Such compensation shall be determined by establishing an equitable balance between the public interest and the interests of those affected. In case of dispute concerning the amount of compensation, recourse may be had to the Imperial Court of Justice.

§ 16

Right of Petition and Complaint

Every person shall have the right individually or jointly with others to address written requests or complaints to competent authorities, to the representative assembly or to the Sovereign. A complaint to higher-ranking bodies or to bodies with the power to issue directives is likewise permissible. A formal reply to the complaint must be given within a period of 72 hours.

§ 17

Forfeiture of Fundamental Rights

Whoever abuses the freedom of expression, in particular the freedom of the press, the freedom of teaching, the freedom of assembly, the freedom of association, the privacy of correspondence, posts and telecommunications or the right of property in order to combat the fundamental order of the monarchy shall forfeit these fundamental rights. This forfeiture and its extent shall be declared by the Imperial Court of Justice.

§ 18

Hierarchy of Legal Norms

The following hierarchy applies to all authorities and citizens of the Empire:

  1. 1. Imperial Charter
  2. 2. Rules of procedure of imperial bodies
  3. 3. Imperial laws and decrees
  4. 4. Charter of a constituent state
  5. 5. Rules of procedure of state bodies
  6. 6. State laws
  7. 7. Ordinances of authorities
§ 19

Amendment of the Fundamental Rights

The articles set out here in (I. Fundamental Rights) may only be amended by a unanimous majority of the Imperial Diet, the Senate and the Sovereign.

§ 20

(vacant)

Section II

The Empire and the Constituent States

§ 1

Form of State

The Imperium Germanicum is a constitutional monarchy organised as a federal state.

§ 2

State Authority

All state authority emanates from the Sovereign. It is exercised by the Imperial Diet in votes and through special organs of legislation, executive power and jurisdiction.

§ 3

Commitment to Law and Justice

Legislation is bound by the constitutional order; the executive and the judiciary are bound by statute and law.

§ 4

Right of Resistance

All citizens have the right to resist anyone who undertakes to abolish this order, if no other remedy is possible.

§ 5

Imperial Capital

The imperial capital of the Imperium Germanicum is Greater Berlin. The representation of the state as a whole in the capital is the task of the Sovereign. Details are regulated by imperial laws.

§ 6

Imperial Flag

The imperial flag is gold with a black, double-headed eagle.

§ 7

Transfer of Sovereign Powers

The Empire may transfer sovereign rights to intergovernmental institutions by law.

§ 8

Protection of the Monarchy

Acts tending to and undertaken with the intent to disturb the peaceful coexistence of the citizens, especially to prepare a conspiracy against the leadership, to shatter the monarchy or to overthrow the Sovereign, shall be unconstitutional. They shall be made a criminal offence.

§ 9

Insignia and Decorations

All insignia, badges and decorations bearing the symbols of the Empire or one of its constituent states are the property of the Imperium Germanicum and are issued to its citizens only on loan.

§ 10

Merchant Fleet

All imperial-royal merchant ships form a single merchant fleet.

§ 11

Reorganisation of the Imperial Territory

The imperial territory may be reorganised to ensure that the constituent states, by size and capacity, can effectively fulfil the tasks incumbent upon them. In doing so, regional ties, historical and cultural connections, economic expediency and the requirements of regional and state planning are to be taken into account.

Decisions on this matter are taken by the Sovereign upon application of the Imperial Diet and the Senate.

§ 12

Competence of the Constituent States

The exercise of state powers and the fulfilment of state tasks is a matter for the constituent states, insofar as this Charter provides or permits no other arrangement.

§ 13

Primacy of Imperial Law

Imperial law overrides state law

§ 14

Foreign Relations

The conduct of relations with foreign states is a matter for the Empire.

§ 15

Hearing of the Constituent States

Before a treaty is concluded that affects the particular circumstances of a constituent state, that state is to be heard in good time.

§ 16

Treaties of the Constituent States

Insofar as the constituent states are responsible for legislation, they may, with the consent of the Senate, conclude treaties with foreign states.

The Sovereign's signature is mandatory.

§ 17

Legal and Administrative Assistance

(1) All authorities of the Empire and the constituent states provide each other with legal and administrative assistance.

(2) In order to maintain or restore public security or order, a state in cases of particular importance may call upon forces and facilities of the armed forces to assist its police when without such assistance the police could not fulfil a task, or could do so only with great difficulty. In order to respond to a natural disaster or an especially grave accident, a constituent state may call for the assistance of police forces of other constituent states or of personnel and facilities of other administrative authorities, of the Customs Administration and of the armed forces.

(3) If the natural disaster or accident endangers the territory of more than one constituent state, the Senate, insofar as is necessary to combat the danger, may instruct the governments of the constituent states to place police forces at the disposal of other states, and may deploy units of the Customs Administration and the armed forces to support the police. Measures taken by the Senate pursuant to the first sentence shall be rescinded at any time at the demand of the Imperial Diet, and in any event as soon as the danger is removed.

§ 18

Imperial Execution

If a constituent state fails to fulfil the imperial obligations incumbent upon it under the Charter or another imperial law, or threatens to take military action against the Empire or one of its constituent states, the Sovereign may, with the consent of the Senate, take the necessary measures to compel the constituent state to fulfil its obligations by way of imperial execution.

§ 19

Power of Direction in Imperial Execution

To carry out the imperial execution, the Sovereign or his delegate has the power to issue directives to all constituent states and their authorities.

§ 20

Prohibition of Wars of Aggression

(1) Acts tending to and undertaken with the intent to disturb the peaceful relations between nations, especially to prepare for a war of aggression, shall be unconstitutional. They shall be made a criminal offence.

(2) Weapons designed for warfare may be manufactured, transported or marketed only with the permission of the Senate. Details shall be regulated by an imperial law.

§ 21

Exclusive Legislative Power of the Empire

(1) The Empire shall have exclusive legislative power with respect to:

  1. 1. foreign affairs and the protection of the civilian population;
  2. 2. citizenship in the Empire;
  3. 3. freedom of movement, passports, residence registration and identity cards, immigration, emigration and extradition;
  4. 4. currency, money and coinage, weights and measures, and the determination of standards of time;
  5. 5. the unity of the customs and trading area, treaties regarding commerce and navigation, the free movement of goods, and the exchange of goods and payments with foreign countries, including customs and border protection;
  6. 5a. the protection of imperial cultural assets against removal from the country;
  7. 6. air transport;
  8. 6a. the operation of railways, the construction, maintenance and operation of railway lines of the Empire, and the levying of charges for the use of these lines;
  9. 7. postal and telecommunication services;
  10. 8. the legal relations of persons employed by the Empire and by imperial corporations under public law;
  11. 9. industrial property rights, copyrights and publishing;
  12. 9a. the protection by the Imperial Police Authority against the dangers of international terrorism and in cases where a danger extending beyond one state exists
  13. 10. cooperation between the Empire and the constituent states
    1. a) in the police authority,
    2. b) to protect the fundamental order, the existence and the security of the Empire or of a constituent state (Imperial Security Office), and
    3. c) to protect against activities within the imperial territory which, by the use of force or preparations for the use of force, endanger the external interests of the Imperium Germanicum, as well as international action to combat crime;
  14. 11. statistics for imperial purposes;
  15. 12. the law on weapons and explosives;
  16. 13. benefits for persons disabled by war and for dependants of deceased war victims as well as assistance to former prisoners of war;
  17. 14. the production and utilisation of nuclear energy for peaceful purposes, the construction and operation of facilities serving such purposes, protection against hazards arising from the release of nuclear energy or from ionising radiation, and the disposal of radioactive substances.
Section III

The Imperial Diet

§ 1

Composition

The members of the Imperial Diet consist of the heads of the constituent states together with their delegations. They are representatives of their member state, not bound by orders or instructions, and responsible only to their conscience and their loyalty to the Sovereign. The Crown Chancellor represents the Empire in his capacity as head of the Imperial Government.

§ 2

Term of Mandate

Subject to the following provisions, the Imperial Diet is empowered for half a year. Its session ends with the convening of a new Imperial Diet. The new mandate is issued no earlier than one week and no later than 2 weeks after the election of the Sovereign.

§ 3

First Convening

The Imperial Diet convenes for the first time with the Sovereign's speech from the throne before each session.

§ 4

Sittings and Convening

The Imperial Diet determines the adjournment and resumption of its sittings. The President of the Imperial Diet may convene it earlier. He is obliged to do so if one third of its members, the Sovereign or the Senate so demand.

§ 5

Presidium

The Sovereign appoints the President of the Imperial Diet, his deputies and the secretaries. The President's term of office lasts one session, usually half a year. Immediate or later reappointment by the Sovereign is possible.

§ 6

Domestic Authority

The President exercises domestic authority and police power in the building of the Imperial Diet. No search or seizure may take place on the premises of the Imperial Diet without his permission.

§ 7

Complaint

An appeal against a decision of the Imperial Diet may be lodged with the Imperial Court of Justice.

§ 8

Right of Access and Audience

The members of the Senate and of the imperial court, as well as their delegates, have access to all sittings of the Imperial Diet and its committees. They must be heard at any time.

§ 9

Amendment of the Charter

The Imperial Diet may amend articles of this Charter, with the exception of (I. Fundamental Rights), by a majority of three quarters of the votes cast. Such resolutions require the consent of the Senate, likewise by a majority of three quarters of the votes cast, and the consent of the Sovereign.

§ 10

Submission to the Senate

Resolutions and decisions of the Imperial Diet must be submitted to the Senate within 20 days at the latest.

Section IV

The Imperial Senate

§ 1

Duty

Through the Imperial Senate, the 9 Electorates, and thus the 9 Prince-Electors, take part in the legislation and administration of the Empire.

§ 2

Composition

The Senate consists of the 9 heads of the 9 Electorates of the Empire; they are appointed upon taking office and recalled upon leaving it. They may be represented by other members of their court.

§ 3

Presidency

The Senate is chaired by the Imperial Ombudsman, who as Hand of the Emperor and primus inter pares forms the link between the Senate and the Sovereign. In the absence of the Sovereign he performs the duties of the Sovereign on an acting basis.

§ 4

Convening

In his function as Hand of the Emperor, the Imperial Ombudsman convenes the Senate. He must convene it if the representatives of at least two Electorates or the Sovereign so demand.

§ 5

Appointment and Dismissal of the Prince-Electors

The 9 Prince-Electors are appointed by the Sovereign on the proposal of the Hand of the Emperor.

Prince-Electors may be dismissed, likewise by the Sovereign, with a three-quarters majority of the Senate.

§ 6

Appointment of the Imperial Ombudsman

The Imperial Ombudsman is formally appointed or dismissed by the Sovereign as his Hand. The term of office of the Hand of the Emperor is tied to that of the Sovereign, usually one year. Reappointment by the Sovereign takes place following his election.

§ 7

Guidelines of the Senate

The Imperial Ombudsman determines the guidelines of the Senate in consultation with the Sovereign and bears responsibility for them. Within these guidelines the Imperial Ombudsman directs the Senate Administration on his own responsibility.

§ 8

Power of Command

The Sovereign holds the power of command over the armed forces.

In practice this task is performed by the Chiefs of Staff.

§ 9

Oath of Office of Officials

All officials of the Empire, with the exception of imperial judges, take the following oath of office upon their installation:

“I swear that I will dedicate my efforts to the well-being of His Imperial and Royal Majesty Alexander IX, promote his welfare, protect him from harm, uphold and defend the Charter and the laws of the Empire, perform my duties conscientiously, and do justice to all who would seek to overthrow our Emperor. So help me God.”

The oath may also be taken without a religious affirmation and, should a new Sovereign be elected, also applies to the next Sovereign.

§ 10

Additional Obligation

For officials who are additionally in the service of one of the constituent states, the oath may contain an additional obligation to the head of the respective member state.

§ 11

Term of Office of the Prince-Electors

The term of office of the Prince-Electors is tied to their reign, usually for life. The term of office of a Prince-Elector ends with his death or through withdrawal of the electoral dignity by the Senate itself; such a motion requires three quarters of the votes cast.

The Imperial Court of Justice may declare a Prince-Elector's office void if it finds that the Prince-Elector has violated the Charter or his official duties.

The title of a Prince-Elector is not hereditary and, after the death of a Prince-Elector, is awarded anew in accordance with § 5. The only exception here is § 6

Section V

The Sovereign

§ 1

Position and Election

(1) The Sovereign is the head of state and commander-in-chief of the armed forces of the Imperium Germanicum; he may de facto entrust other office-holders with the exercise of his duties or with command over the armed forces as he pleases.

(2) The Sovereign is elected by the Senate without debate. Every Prince-Elector entitled to vote is eligible.

§ 2

Term of Office

The office of Sovereign lasts one year. Subsequent re-election is permitted without limit.

§ 3

Coronation

An official coronation of the Sovereign takes place only upon the first election; upon re-election, homage is paid to the Sovereign in another manner befitting his rank.

§ 4

Convening for the Election

The Senate convenes no later than thirty days after the end of the Sovereign's term. The election is convened by the Imperial Ombudsman.

§ 5

Electoral Procedure and Vacancy of the Throne

Whoever receives the votes of a majority of the members of the Senate is elected.

If this majority is not reached after 10 ballots, a run-off between the two Prince-Electors with the most votes decides; the person elected must then receive the most votes cast.

During this period of vacancy of the throne, the Imperial Ombudsman temporarily assumes the leadership of the Empire; if he too is unable to perform his duties, the longest-serving Prince-Elector takes over.

§ 6

Oath of Office

Upon taking office, the Sovereign takes the following oath before the assembled members of the Senate and the Imperial Diet:

“I swear that I will devote my strength to the welfare of the people and the Empire, increase its benefit, avert harm from it, uphold and defend the Charter and the laws of the Empire, conscientiously fulfil my duties and administer justice to everyone. So help me God.”

The oath may also be taken without a religious affirmation.

§ 7

Representation

The sovereign rights and duties of the Sovereign are exercised by the Imperial Ombudsman in the event of his incapacity or death.

§ 8

Representation under International Law

The Sovereign represents the Empire under international law. He concludes treaties with foreign states on behalf of the Empire. He accredits and receives envoys. He brings treaties and laws into force by his signature.

§ 9

Treaties Requiring Consent

Treaties that regulate the political relations of the Empire or relate to subjects of imperial legislation require the consent of the Senate. In the case of executive agreements, the provisions concerning the Imperial Government apply mutatis mutandis.

§ 10

Power of Appointment

The Sovereign appoints and dismisses the Imperial Judges, imperial officials, officers and non-commissioned officers, unless otherwise provided by law.

§ 11

Power of Pardon

In individual cases he exercises the right of pardon for the Empire.

§ 12

Impeachment

The Senate may impeach the Sovereign before the Imperial Court of Justice for wilful violation of the Charter or failure to fulfil his duties. The motion to bring the charge must be made by at least one quarter of the members of the Senate. The resolution to bring the charge requires a majority of two thirds of the members of the Senate. The charge is represented by a delegate of the accusing body.

§ 13

Removal from Office

If the Imperial Court of Justice finds that the monarch is guilty of an intentional violation of the Charter or of disregarding his official duties, it may declare that he has forfeited his office. After the impeachment has been preferred, it may issue an interim order preventing him from exercising his functions.

§ 14

Titles

By taking office, the Sovereign and his consort are entitled to the following titles, which he receives ex officio, in the masculine and feminine form.

Sovereign
His Imperial and Royal Majesty (Alexander IX), by the Grace of God Emperor of the Imperium Germanicum, King of Apulia, (King of the Westerwald Union), Archduke of Austria and (Prince of Sayen-Wittgenstein-Sayen)
Consort
Her Imperial and Royal Majesty (Francesca), by the Grace of God Empress of the Imperium Germanicum, Queen of Apulia, (Queen of the Westerwald Union), Archduchess of Austria and (Princess of Sayen-Wittgenstein-Sayen)
§ 15

Form of address

The Sovereign and his consort are, as a rule, to be addressed as follows: long form; short form, letter abbreviation.

SovereignYour/His {Imperial and Royal} MajestyMajestyHIRM/HM
ConsortYour/Her {Imperial and Royal} MajestyMajestyHIRM/HM
§ 16

Legislative Procedure

Imperial laws pass first through the Imperial Diet, then the Senate and then the Sovereign; in case of further disagreement a ruling of the Imperial Court of Justice is required, whose rulings are final and can only be overturned by the Court itself.

The Sovereign is responsible for the promulgation of new laws, decrees and edicts in the Imperial Law Gazette.

§ 17

Right of Veto

The Sovereign has a general right of veto against resolutions of the Senate; however, the Sovereign must lodge this veto within 48 hours and justify it by articles of the Charter within 72 hours. The Imperial Court of Justice must examine this justification within 48 hours.

Likewise, the Senate has a right of veto over the decisions of the Imperial Diet, subject to the same conditions.

§ 18

Decrees

The Sovereign may issue temporary decrees without the Imperial Diet; these decrees must be confirmed by the Senate within one month at the latest, or they are automatically dissolved.

Decrees may not violate the fundamental rights.

Section VI

The Imperial Government

§ 1

Composition

The Imperial Government consists of the Crown Chancellor and the heads of the authorities.

§ 2

Election of the Crown Chancellor

(1) The Crown Chancellor is elected by the Imperial Diet on the proposal of the Sovereign.

(2) The person who obtains the votes of a majority of the members of the Imperial Diet is elected. The person elected must be appointed by the Sovereign.

(3) If the person proposed is not elected, the Imperial Diet may propose a counter-candidate within 5 days of the ballot.

(4) If no election takes place within this period, the Sovereign designates a candidate of his choice.

§ 3

Heads of Authorities and Oath of Office

(1) The heads of the authorities are appointed and dismissed by the Sovereign on the proposal of the Chancellor.

(2) All officials of the Empire, with the exception of imperial judges, take the following oath of office upon their installation:

“I swear that I will dedicate my efforts to the well-being of His Imperial and Royal Majesty Alexander IX, promote his welfare, protect him from harm, uphold and defend the Charter and the laws of the Empire, perform my duties conscientiously, and do justice to all who would seek to overthrow our Emperor. So help me God.”

The oath may also be taken without a religious affirmation and, should a new Sovereign be elected, also applies to the next Sovereign.

§ 4

Policy Guidelines

The Chancellor determines the guidelines of policy after consultation with the Sovereign and bears responsibility for them. Within these guidelines each head of authority conducts the affairs of his department independently and on his own responsibility. The Imperial Government decides on differences of opinion between the heads of authorities. The Crown Chancellor conducts its business in accordance with rules of procedure adopted by the Imperial Government and approved by the Imperial Diet.

§ 5

Incompatibility

The Crown Chancellor and the heads of authorities may not be rulers of a state or Prince-Electors, and may not belong to the management or, without the consent of the Imperial Diet, to the supervisory board of an enterprise conducted for profit.

§ 6

Vote of No Confidence

(1) The Imperial Diet may express its lack of confidence in the Crown Chancellor only by electing a successor by a three-quarters majority of its members and requesting the Sovereign to dismiss the Chancellor. The Sovereign may comply with the request and appoint the person elected.

(2) Forty-eight hours must elapse between the motion and the election.

§ 7

Deputy and End of Term

(1) The Crown Chancellor appoints a head of authority as his deputy.

(2) The term of office of the Crown Chancellor lasts half a year. The office of the Chancellor ends in any event with the convening of a new Imperial Diet.

(3) At the request of the Sovereign the Crown Chancellor, and at the request of the Chancellor or the Sovereign a head of authority, is obliged to continue to conduct the affairs of office until a successor is appointed.

§ 8

Implementation of Resolutions

Resolutions and decisions of the Imperial Government must be implemented by the authorities of the Empire and the constituent states within 7 days at the latest; otherwise the Empire is empowered to carry out the imperial execution or otherwise authorised to enforce order and law. For this purpose the armed forces, the police authority, the Customs Administration and civil protection are at the disposal of the Imperial Government.

Section VII

Jurisdiction

§ 1

Judicial Power

Judicial power is vested in the judges; it is exercised by the Imperial Court of Justice and by the courts of the constituent states.

§ 2

Position of the Imperial Court of Justice

The Imperial Court of Justice is a court of the Empire that is independent of all other imperial bodies and bound only to the Sovereign.

§ 3

Composition

The Imperial Court of Justice consists of 5 Imperial Judges, one of whom serves as Chief Justice, and other members.

§ 4

Interpretation of the Charter

The Imperial Court of Justice decides on the interpretation of this Charter in the event of disputes over the scope of the rights and duties of a supreme imperial organ or other parties who are endowed with rights of their own by this Charter or by the rules of procedure of a supreme imperial organ;

§ 5

Binding Effect

The decisions of the Imperial Court of Justice bind the constitutional bodies of the Empire and the constituent states as well as all courts and authorities. An imperial law determines in which cases its decisions have the force of law.

§ 6

Reversal of Decisions

Decisions made by the Imperial Court of Justice can be overturned only by the court itself.

§ 7

Independence of the Judges

The judges are subject only to the law and the Sovereign.

§ 8

Judicial Oath

All judges must take the following oath at the beginning of their term of office:

“I swear to exercise the office of judge faithful to His Imperial and Royal Majesty (Alexander IX) and faithful to the law, to judge to the best of my knowledge and conscience without respect of persons, and to serve only truth and justice, so help me God.”
§ 9

Oath without Religious Affirmation

The oath may be taken without the words “so help me God” and, upon the election of a new Monarch, also applies to the new Sovereign.

§ 10

Additional Obligation

For judges in the service of the constituent states, the oath may contain an additional obligation to the head of the respective member state.

§ 11

Term of Office and Rules of Procedure

(1) The term of office of the members of the Imperial Court of Justice lasts one year. After their term expires, the judges continue to conduct their official business until their successor is appointed. Immediate or later reappointment by the Sovereign is possible without limit.

(2) The Imperial Court of Justice adopts rules of procedure, which are resolved by the plenary.

§ 12

Jurisdiction

The Imperial Court of Justice rules:

  1. 1. on the interpretation of this Charter in the event of disputes concerning the extent of the rights and duties of a supreme imperial body or of other parties vested with rights of their own by this Charter or by the rules of procedure of a supreme imperial body;
  2. 2. in the event of disagreements or doubts concerning the formal or substantive compatibility of imperial or state law with this Charter, or the compatibility of state law with other imperial law, on application of the Senate, of a state government or of one quarter of the members of the Imperial Diet;
  3. 2a. in the event of disagreements as to whether a law meets the requirements of Article 72 (2), on application of the Senate, of a state government or of the representative assembly of a constituent state;
  4. 3. in the event of disagreements concerning the rights and duties of the Empire and the constituent states, especially in the execution of imperial law by the constituent states and in the exercise of imperial supervision;
  5. 4. on other disputes under public law between the Empire and the constituent states, between different constituent states or within a constituent state, unless there is recourse to another court;
  6. 4a. on constitutional complaints, which may be filed by any person alleging that one of their fundamental rights or one of their rights under Article 20 (4) or Articles 33, 38, 101, 103 or 104 has been infringed by public authority;
  7. 4b. on constitutional complaints filed by municipalities or associations of municipalities on the ground that their right to self-government under Article 28 has been infringed by a law; in the case of state laws, however, only where a complaint cannot be filed with the state constitutional court;
  8. 4c. on complaints of associations against their non-recognition as an association
  9. 5. in the other instances provided for in this Charter.
§ 13

Execution of Resolutions and Judgments

Resolutions and judgments of the Imperial Court of Justice must be carried out by the end of a period set by the Court, which must, however, be at least 48 hours.

§ 14

Lèse-majesté

The offence of lèse-majesté must be prosecuted by the Sovereign or an authorised office-holder.

§ 15

Rights before the Courts

(1) In the courts every person shall be entitled to a hearing in accordance with law.

(2) An act may be punished only if it was defined by a law as a criminal offence before the act was committed. Acts in the area of high treason are an exception.

(3) No person may be punished for the same act more than once under the general criminal laws.

§ 16

Abolition of the Death Penalty

The death penalty is abolished.

Section VIII

Martial Law

§ 1

Determination

The determination that the imperial territory is being attacked by force of arms, or that such an attack is imminent (martial law), is made by the Monarch with the consent of the Senate.

§ 2

Promulgation

The determination is proclaimed by the Monarch. If this is not possible in time, proclamation takes place in another manner; the Monarch must make it up as soon as circumstances permit.

§ 3

Transfer of the Power of Command

With the promulgation of martial law, special powers of command over the armed forces of all constituent states pass to the Sovereign; the Sovereign receives the necessary special powers with the consent of the Senate.

§ 4

Powers under Martial Law

Under martial law, insofar as circumstances require, the Sovereign may

  • Deploy the armed forces throughout the imperial territory;
  • issue directives not only to the Imperial Government but also to the governments of the constituent states and, if he considers it urgent, to the authorities of the constituent states, and delegate this power to representatives to be designated by him.
  • temporarily dissolve the Imperial Diet and govern provisionally with the Senate alone.

The Senate votes at least every 4 weeks on whether the state must be maintained; otherwise martial law ends automatically. Ordinarily, the Sovereign himself ends the state after the danger has passed, for example upon the signing of a peace treaty or agreement.
