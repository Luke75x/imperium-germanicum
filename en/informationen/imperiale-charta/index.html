The Imperial Charter

Preamble

Preamble

Conscious of his responsibility before God, mankind and the Sovereign,

inspired by the will to serve the peace of the world as an equal member in a united Empire, the people, by virtue of its power, has given itself this Charter.

The subjects in the 9 Electorates and dozens of further territorial bodies have completed the unity and freedom of the Empire. This Charter thus applies to the entire people of the Imperium Germanicum.

Section I

Fundamental Rights

§ 1

Human dignity is inviolable. To respect and protect it is the duty of all state authority.

§ 2

Everyone has the right to the free development of their personality, insofar as they do not violate the rights of others and do not offend against the order of the Charter or fall under lèse-majesté.

§ 3

Men and women have equal rights. The state promotes the actual implementation of equal rights for women and men and works towards the elimination of existing disadvantages.

§ 4

No one may be disadvantaged or favoured because of their sex, descent, race, language, homeland and origin, faith, or religious or political views. No one may be disadvantaged because of a disability.

§ 5

Freedom of faith and conscience and freedom of religious and ideological creed are inviolable.

§ 6

Art and science, research and teaching are free. Freedom of teaching does not release anyone from loyalty to the Sovereign.

§ 7

All subjects have the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, without notification or permission.

§ 8

Associations whose purposes or activities contravene the criminal laws, or which are directed against the Sovereign or against the idea of monarchy, are prohibited.

§ 9

In principle, anyone who has been approved by the Immigration and Migration Authority and has signed an application for membership can become a citizen of the Imperium Germanicum.

§ 10

All citizens of the Imperium Germanicum pay a tax as a membership fee to finance the state.

Details are governed by a resolution of the Imperial Diet.

§ 11

Every citizen remains free to leave the Imperium Germanicum at any time without notice.

Section II

The Empire and the Constituent States

§ 1

The Imperium Germanicum is a constitutional monarchy organised as a federal state.

§ 2

All state authority emanates from the Sovereign. It is exercised by the Imperial Diet in votes and through special organs of legislation, executive power and jurisdiction.

§ 3

Legislation is bound by the constitutional order; the executive and the judiciary are bound by statute and law.

§ 4

All citizens have the right to resist anyone who undertakes to abolish this order, if no other remedy is possible.

§ 5

The imperial capital of the Imperium Germanicum is Greater Berlin. The representation of the state as a whole in the capital is the task of the Sovereign. Details are regulated by imperial laws.

§ 6

The imperial flag is gold with a black, double-headed eagle.

§ 7

The Empire may transfer sovereign rights to intergovernmental institutions by law.

§ 8

Acts that are suited to, and carried out with the intention of, disturbing the peaceful coexistence of citizens, in particular preparing a plot against the leadership, smashing the monarchy or overthrowing the Sovereign, are unconstitutional. They are to be made punishable.

§ 9

All insignia, badges and decorations bearing the symbols of the Empire or one of its constituent states are the property of the Imperium Germanicum and are issued to its citizens only on loan.

§ 10

All imperial-royal merchant ships form a single merchant fleet.

§ 11

The imperial territory may be reorganised to ensure that the constituent states, by size and capacity, can effectively fulfil the tasks incumbent upon them. In doing so, regional ties, historical and cultural connections, economic expediency and the requirements of regional and state planning are to be taken into account.

Decisions on this are made by the Sovereign upon application of the Imperial Diet and the Senate.

§ 12

The exercise of state powers and the fulfilment of state tasks is a matter for the constituent states, insofar as this Charter provides or permits no other arrangement.

§ 13

Imperial law overrides state law

§ 14

The conduct of relations with foreign states is a matter for the Empire.

§ 15

Before a treaty is concluded that affects the particular circumstances of a constituent state, that state is to be heard in good time.

§ 16

Insofar as the constituent states are responsible for legislation, they may, with the consent of the Senate, conclude treaties with foreign states.

The Sovereign's signature is mandatory.

§ 17

All authorities of the Empire and the constituent states provide each other with legal and administrative assistance.

§ 18

If a constituent state fails to fulfil the imperial obligations incumbent upon it under the Charter or another imperial law, or threatens to take military action against the Empire or one of its constituent states, the Sovereign may, with the consent of the Senate, take the necessary measures to compel the constituent state to fulfil its obligations by way of imperial execution.

§ 19

To carry out the imperial execution, the Sovereign or his delegate has the power to issue directives to all constituent states and their authorities.

Section III

The Imperial Diet

§ 1

The deputies of the Imperial Diet consist of the heads of the constituent states plus their delegations. They represent their constituent state, are not bound by mandates or instructions, and are subject only to their conscience and to loyalty to the Sovereign.

§ 2

Subject to the following provisions, the Imperial Diet is mandated for 1 year. Its session period ends when a new Imperial Diet convenes. The new mandate is issued no earlier than one week and no later than 2 days before the start of the session period.

§ 3

The Imperial Diet convenes for the first time before each session period with the Sovereign's speech from the throne.

§ 4

The Imperial Diet determines the close and the resumption of its sittings. The Chairman of the Imperial Diet may convene it earlier. He is obliged to do so if one third of the members, the Sovereign or the Senate demand it.

§ 5

The Sovereign appoints the Chairman of the Imperial Diet, his deputy and the secretaries.

§ 6

The Chairman exercises domestic authority in the building of the Imperial Diet. No search or seizure may take place in the premises of the Imperial Diet without his permission.

§ 7

An appeal against a decision of the Imperial Diet may be lodged with the Imperial Court of Justice.

§ 8

The members of the Senate and of the imperial court, as well as their delegates, have access to all sittings of the Imperial Diet and its committees. They must be heard at any time.

Section IV

The Imperial Senate

§ 1

Through the Imperial Senate, the 9 Electorates, and thus the 9 Prince-Electors, take part in the legislation and administration of the Empire.

§ 2

The Senate consists of the 9 heads of the 9 Electorates of the Empire; they are appointed upon taking office and recalled upon leaving it. They may be represented by other members of their court.

§ 3

Within its own structure the Senate has 3 special offices, beginning with the Imperial Ombudsman, who chairs the Senate and, during a vacancy of the throne, temporarily takes over the Sovereign's official duties; he is also widely styled the Hand of the Emperor.

The other two offices are appointed by the Imperial Ombudsman and comprise the Arch-Treasurer, who is responsible for the administration and proper management of His Majesty's treasury, and the Arch-Marshal, who serves as ceremonial patron of the Imperial Guard of Greater Berlin and is the Chief Recorder of the Empire.

§ 4

As Hand of the Emperor, the Imperial Ombudsman convenes the Senate. He must convene it if the representatives of at least two Electorates or the Sovereign demand it.

§ 5

The 9 Prince-Electors and all Arch-Offices are appointed by the Sovereign on the proposal of the Hand of the Emperor.

Arch-Offices may be dismissed by the Sovereign on the proposal of the Imperial Ombudsman, and all other Prince-Electors by a three-quarters majority of the Senate, likewise by the Sovereign.

§ 6

The Prince-Elector of the Palatinate is the only one of the 9 Prince-Electors with a permanent seat in the Senate; he assumes the position of Imperial Ombudsman ex officio and is formally appointed or dismissed as the Sovereign's Hand by the Sovereign.

§ 7

The Sovereign determines the guidelines of policy and bears responsibility for them. Within these guidelines the Imperial Ombudsman directs the Senate administration independently and on his own responsibility.

§ 8

The Sovereign holds the power of command over the armed forces.

In practice this task is performed by the Chiefs of Staff.

§ 9

All government officials except Imperial Judges take the following oath of office upon their installation:

“I swear that I will devote my strength to the welfare of His Imperial and Royal Majesty Alexander IX., increase his benefit, avert harm from him, uphold and defend the Charter and the laws of the Empire, conscientiously fulfil my duties and administer justice to anyone who is able to overthrow our Emperor. So help me God.”

The oath may also be taken without a religious affirmation and, should a new Sovereign be elected, also applies to the next Sovereign.

§ 10

For officials who additionally serve one of the constituent states, the oath may contain an additional pledge to the head of the respective constituent state.

§ 11

For the Senate, the withdrawal of a Prince-Elector's electoral dignity is possible only by a three-quarters majority of the Senate, by a decision of the Imperial Court of Justice, or by the death of the officeholder.

In addition, the consent of the Sovereign is required.

Section V

The Sovereign

§ 1

The Sovereign is elected by the Senate without debate. Any eligible Prince-Elector may be elected.

§ 2

The office of Sovereign lasts one year. Subsequent re-election is permitted without limit.

§ 3

An official coronation of the Sovereign takes place only at the first election; at re-elections, homage is paid to the Sovereign in another manner befitting his rank.

§ 4

The Senate convenes no later than thirty days after the end of the Sovereign's term. The election is convened by the Imperial Ombudsman.

§ 5

Whoever receives the votes of a majority of the members of the Senate is elected.

If this majority is not reached, the procedure is to be repeated until the required majority is reached.

During this period of vacancy of the throne, the Imperial Ombudsman temporarily assumes the leadership of the Empire; if he too is unable to perform his duties, the longest-serving Prince-Elector takes over.

§ 6

Upon taking office, the Monarch swears the following oath before the assembled members of the Senate and the Imperial Diet:

“I swear that I will devote my strength to the welfare of the people and the Empire, increase its benefit, avert harm from it, uphold and defend the Charter and the laws of the Empire, conscientiously fulfil my duties and administer justice to everyone. So help me God.”

The oath may also be taken without a religious affirmation.

§ 7

The sovereign rights and duties of the Sovereign are exercised by the Imperial Ombudsman in the event of his incapacity or death.

§ 8

The Monarch represents the Empire under international law. He concludes treaties with foreign states in the name of the Empire. He accredits and receives envoys. He brings treaties and laws into force by his signature.

§ 9

Treaties that regulate the political relations of the Empire or relate to matters of imperial legislation require the consent of the Senate. For administrative agreements the provisions on imperial administration apply accordingly.

§ 10

The Sovereign appoints and dismisses the Imperial Judges, imperial officials, officers and non-commissioned officers, unless otherwise provided by law.

§ 11

In individual cases he exercises the right of pardon for the Empire.

§ 12

The Senate may impeach the Sovereign before the Imperial Court of Justice for wilful violation of the Charter or failure to fulfil his duties. The motion to bring the charge must be made by at least one quarter of the members of the Senate. The resolution to bring the charge requires a majority of two thirds of the members of the Senate. The charge is represented by a delegate of the accusing body.

§ 13

If the Imperial Court of Justice finds that the Monarch is guilty of a wilful violation of the Charter or of disregarding his official duties, it may declare him to have forfeited his office. By interim order it may, after the charge has been brought, determine that he is prevented from exercising his office.

§ 14

Upon taking office, the Sovereign and his consort are entitled to the following titles, which they receive ex officio, in the masculine and feminine form.

Sovereign
His Imperial and Royal Majesty Alexander IX., by the Grace of God Emperor of the Imperium Germanicum, King of Apulia, King of the Westerwald Union, Archduke of Austria and Prince of Sayn-Wittgenstein-Sayn
Consort
Her Imperial and Royal Majesty Francesca, by the Grace of God Empress of the Imperium Germanicum, Queen of Apulia, Queen of the Westerwald Union, Archduchess of Austria and Princess of Sayn-Wittgenstein-Sayn
§ 15

The Sovereign and his consort are, as a rule, to be addressed as follows – long form, short form, letter abbreviation:

SovereignYour/His {Imperial and Royal} MajestyMajestyHIRM/HM
ConsortYour/Her {Imperial and Royal} MajestyMajestyHIRM/HM
§ 16

Resolutions and decrees pass first through the Imperial Diet, then the Senate and then the Sovereign; in the event of further disagreement a ruling of the Imperial Court of Justice is required, whose rulings are final and can be overturned only by the court itself.

§ 17

The Sovereign has a general right of veto against resolutions of the Senate; this veto must, however, be justified by the Charter.

Likewise, the Senate has a right of veto over the decisions of the Imperial Diet, subject to the same conditions.

§ 18

The Sovereign may issue temporary decrees without the Senate; these decrees must be confirmed by the Senate within one month at the latest, or they are automatically revoked.

Section VI

Jurisdiction

§ 1

Judicial power is vested in the judges; it is exercised by the Imperial Court of Justice and by the courts of the constituent states.

§ 2

The Imperial Court of Justice is a court of the Empire that is independent of all other imperial organs and committed to the Sovereign.

§ 3

The Imperial Court of Justice consists of 5 Imperial Judges, one of whom serves as Chief Justice, and other members.

§ 4

The Imperial Court of Justice decides on the interpretation of this Charter in the event of disputes over the scope of the rights and duties of a supreme imperial organ or other parties who are endowed with rights of their own by this Charter or by the rules of procedure of a supreme imperial organ;

§ 5

The decisions of the Imperial Court of Justice bind the state organs of the Empire and of the constituent states as well as all courts and authorities. An imperial law determines in which cases its decisions have the force of law.

§ 6

Decisions made by the Imperial Court of Justice can be overturned only by the court itself.

§ 7

Judges are independent and subject only to the law and the Sovereign.

§ 8

All judges must take the following oath at the beginning of their term of office:

“I swear to exercise the office of judge faithfully to His Imperial and Royal Majesty Alexander IX. and faithfully to the law, to judge to the best of my knowledge and conscience without regard to the person, and to serve only truth and justice, so help me God.”
§ 9

The oath may be taken without the words “so help me God” and, upon the election of a new Monarch, also applies to the new Sovereign.

§ 10

For judges in the service of the constituent states, the oath may contain an additional pledge to the head of the respective constituent state.

Section VII

Martial Law

§ 1

The determination that the imperial territory is being attacked by force of arms, or that such an attack is imminent (martial law), is made by the Monarch with the consent of the Senate.

§ 2

The determination is proclaimed by the Monarch. If this is not possible in time, proclamation takes place in another manner; the Monarch must make it up as soon as circumstances permit.

§ 3

With the proclamation of martial law, special powers of command over the armed forces of all constituent states pass to the Sovereign; the Sovereign receives the necessary special powers by consent of the Senate.

§ 4

Under martial law, insofar as circumstances require, the Sovereign may

  1. Deploy the armed forces throughout the imperial territory;
  2. Issue instructions, in addition to the imperial administration, also to the governments of the constituent states and, if he deems it urgent, to the authorities of the constituent states, and delegate this authority to representatives to be appointed by him.
  3. Temporarily dissolve the Imperial Diet and govern temporarily with the Senate alone.

The Senate votes at least every 4 weeks on whether the state of affairs must be maintained.
